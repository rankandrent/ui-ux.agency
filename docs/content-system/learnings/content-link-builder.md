## Lessons (learned from runs, QA, audits and results)

- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency): WebFetch was EGRESS_BLOCKED for nngroup.com, gov.uk, hhs.gov, iso.org and insightsassociation.org. A WebSearch restricted with `allowed_domains` to the source's own domain returned the exact URL plus snippets from the page itself, while open searches mostly returned third-party summaries. Rule: when fetch is blocked, verify with a domain-restricted search first and cite the URL that search returns; if the brief's URL does not show up but a canonical one does (HHS `/guidance/document/...` vs `/hipaa/for-professionals/covered-entities/sample-business-associate-agreement-provisions/`), cite the canonical one and note the swap in the brief.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency): The writer left 4 hub markers, but RULES allows only 1–2 hub links and one link per target. Rule: keep the earliest hub marker (it has to fall within the first 300 words, using the hub's `anchor`) plus one later marker that the brief specifically asks for (here the pricing link). Delete the extra hub markers and leave their sentences as plain text, and put the contact link on the CTA sentence itself instead of a standalone marker.
- 2026-10-05 (choose-ux-research-agency): The snippet for the GOV.UK 2015 agency post showed it covers large quantitative surveys (1000+ respondents) and the client's remaining duties (brief, objectives, stakeholder buy-in). The writer's paraphrase was vaguer than that. Rule: when a snippet shows the source's real scope, rewrite the cited sentence to match that scope rather than linking a loose paraphrase.
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign): The writer placed 10 internal markers on a BOFU post: 4 to the hub (two of them `#section` fragments), 3 case studies. Fragment links count as hub links, so they count toward the 1–2 hub cap, and case studies are capped at 1–2. Rule: before replacing markers, count them per bucket (hub incl. fragments, case studies, other hub, industry, contact) and cut the excess first. Keep the early hub link and the end-of-post hub link, keep the case study that has a full page over a TODO stub (or the one that is the only proof in its section), and leave the dropped facts as plain text or remove sentences that only existed to carry a link ("Our hub lists…").
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign): usability.gov (archived 2025) did not show up in a domain-restricted search and fetch was blocked. Brooke's JUS "SUS: A Retrospective" turned up by title, but no search returned its URL. The peer-reviewed JMIR 2022 SUS benchmark meta-analysis did come back with a verifiable URL and a snippet (benchmark mean 68). Rule: when an archived gov source has to be replaced, only cite a replacement whose exact URL a search returned. Rewrite the claim to what that snippet supports (here, the benchmark of 68) and drop details the new source does not show (the year 1986).
- 2026-10-06 (saas-product-redesign): The brief listed 6 external sources, but the cap is 5. Two of them (GV change aversion, NN/g "Users hate change") backed the same point. Rule: when the brief exceeds 5 external sources, drop the one that duplicates a claim another source already covers. Keep the primary or original source and use its verified snippet to make the remaining sentence more specific.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer): The brief cited the NN/g how-to page for "1 evaluator about 35%, 3–5 about 75%". A domain-restricted search confirmed the URL but showed only "3–5 people evaluating independently". The figures live on other NN/g pages: 35% on "Technology Transfer", and 20–50% / 74–87% on "Theory Behind Heuristic Evaluations". "About 75%" never appeared. Rule: verify each number on the exact page cited, not just that the URL exists. Cite the page whose snippet shows the figures and rewrite the linked sentence to those figures. Flag any unlinked copies of the old number (takeaways, bold answer, visual markers) to the fact-checker instead of editing them.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer): The same source marker (baymard.com/blog/gpt-ux-audit) appeared twice, in the intro and in the AI section. Rule: link an external source once, on its first claim, and leave later mentions of the same study as plain text ("In Baymard's 2023 test ...") so the post keeps one link per target.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer): The `site:ui-ux.agency` search returned nothing indexed, and `content/home.ts` compare.intro (the most relevant backlink source) renders as plain text in HomeSections.tsx. Rule: before suggesting a backlink, check that the source field renders HTML (`Html` component vs `{d.intro}`). Hub section `body`/`answer` fields render HTML; home `intro` strings do not, so mark those as conditional on a template change.
- 2026-10-07 (ux-audit-agency-vs-freelancer, QA loop 1): after the hub markers were cut to the cap, the post was left with 5 contextual links. QA requires 6–12, so it failed even though onpage (≥ 5) passed. The frontmatter listed `industries: [saas, ecommerce]`, and the body named "an ecommerce store", but only /industries/saas/ was linked. Rule: after you trim to the hub cap, count the body links. If there are fewer than 6, add the missing frontmatter industry pages (or a same-silo post) on a sentence that already names them, before you consider another hub fragment.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi): The writer cited "May 2024" BLS OOH medians ($133,080, $98,090). A bls.gov-restricted search showed the same pages now carry May 2025 data ($135,980 / $134,040 group; $104,000 / $99,520 group). Rule: BLS OOH and OEWS pages are overwritten every year in place. Always re-check the year and figure against today's snippet, update the linked sentence and any derived arithmetic in it (loaded $/hour), and flag downstream copies (worked-example tables) to the fact-checker.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi): The post had 6 external sources, 2 of them sibling BLS OOH profile pages that each backed one median. The BLS OOH group overview (`/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/`) shows both group medians in one table. Rule: when two pages from the same primary publisher back parallel numbers and the cap is exceeded, cite one parent or overview page that shows both (verified on that exact page) instead of dropping a different source's claim.
- 2026-10-08 (design-system-roi): On a BOFU post in a new silo (no sibling posts, no case study for the service), the hub cap (2), one other hub, the frontmatter industry and contact leave only 5 internal links. Rule: do not break the hub cap or invent a case study link to reach QA's 6. Report the 5 as within the BOFU 5–8 range and name the next allowed candidate (here a conditional industry page) for QA.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp): The writer cited FAR 37.101 for the performance-based acquisition definition. An acquisition.gov-restricted search for the definition's exact wording returned FAR 2.101. The 37.101 page only defines hourly rate terms, and in the FAR Overhaul draft 37.101 is "Presolicitation". Rule: for regulations (FAR, CFR), search the quoted definition text, not the section number, and cite the section the snippet shows. Rewrite the sentence to the definition's own words: "results to be achieved", not the writer's "measurable outcomes".
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp): The brief's 18F PDF URL never came back in any guides.18f.gov search. The HTML chapter "Buying custom software development services" did, with a snippet supporting the claim. The PDF's "2024" date could not be confirmed. Rule: when a guide is published both as PDF and as HTML, cite the HTML chapter that a search returned with a matching snippet, and drop publication dates that only the brief asserts.
- 2026-10-09 (web-app-design-rfp): The post had 5 hub markers, one of them a mid-post `#cost` fragment, and the closing sentence said "compare our number with the range on our … page". Rule: when the closing UP link sentence mentions price, make that closing link the `#…-cost` fragment, so it does the fragment's job inside the 2-link cap. When you delete a marker whose sentence points at the link ("our service page shows …"), rewrite the sentence so it no longer refers to a page the reader cannot click.
- 2026-10-10 (is-discovery-phase-worth-it): The brief offered an NN/g video ("many teams are not doing true discovery") for a readout warning that says "no users = not discovery". An nngroup.com-restricted search turned up the companion survey article (discoveries-in-industry-revealed), whose snippet says many discoveries "lack user research and don't involve the right people". Rule: when a brief allows a swap within the same publisher, cite the page whose snippet states the post's specific claim rather than the general one, and record the swap in the brief's `related.externalSources`.
- 2026-10-10 (is-discovery-phase-worth-it): The BOFU post quoted results from 3 case studies (Nitro, Vocable, Digno), which exceeds the 1–2 case-study bucket. The orchestrator asked for all three because each one is the named source of a quoted figure. Rule: a case study that is the only source of a quoted number keeps its link even above the 1–2 cap (honesty rule: numbers need a linkable source). Pay for it by cutting hub fragments, and keep the total inside the funnel range (here 8 = BOFU max).
- 2026-10-10 (is-discovery-phase-worth-it): Searching content/ for backlink sources turned up a strong match in another silo's post (saas-product-redesign: "the discovery step of our SaaS UX design engagement"). Rule: the silo rule works in both directions. Never suggest a backlink from another silo's blog post. Hubs, and case studies whose `services` list includes the post's hub, are the allowed backlink sources.
- 2026-10-11 (mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer): WebFetch of the Apple HIG Buttons page returned only its title, because the page is rendered client-side. In developer.apple.com-restricted searches, the "44x44 pt hit region" sentence showed up only as a quote in an Apple Developer Forums thread. Apple's static "UI Design Dos and Don'ts" page (`/design/tips/`) fetched and states "controls that measure at least 44 points x 44 points so they can be accurately tapped with a finger". Rule: for HIG figures, a forum quote does not count as verification. Cite the Apple page whose own fetched text shows the number, and reword the sentence to that page's wording (here "controls", not "button hit region").
- 2026-10-11 (mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer): Blog H2 ids come from `slugify()` in `app/blog/[slug]/page.tsx`, which turns every apostrophe into a hyphen ("freelancer's" → `freelancer-s`). That differs from GitHub-style slugs. Rule: build a backlink's `#section` jump into a blog post with that function's rules. Hub ids come from `sectionId()` in SemanticSections.tsx, which follows the same pattern.
- 2026-10-11 (mobile-app-design-agency-vs-freelancer): `home.faqs` renders HTML on the homepage, but `/contact/` also shows `home.faqs.slice(0, 6)`. Rule: suggest a homepage FAQ backlink only on index 6 or later, so the link never lands on the contact conversion path. Mark it optional, because those answers feed the FAQPage schema.
- 2026-10-12 (usability-testing-cost-per-participant): The writer used 9 inline external links instead of `<!-- source -->` markers, all of them verifiable. A `<!-- visual -->` marker named the GitLab row, which tied that source to the post. Rule: when you cut to the 5-link cap, keep the links that carry numbers (incentive, fees, rates) and any source a visual marker names. Turn verified qualitative sources (here NN/g older adults and the JMIR 2021 care packages) into named plain-text citations, record them in the brief's `related.removedExternalLinks`, and delete a duplicate figure ($171 video vs $107 article) outright.
- 2026-10-12 (usability-testing-cost-per-participant): The writer's GitLab URL (`/research-operations/participation-gratuities/`) never came back in a handbook.gitlab.com search, while the brief's `/ux-research-coordination/` path did, with all three tiers in the snippet. Writer-supplied inline URLs also drift from the brief. Rule: check each inline URL against the search-returned URL character for character, not only the claim, and cite the returned one. Separately, "$100/hour ranked highest on fill rate" was not in any snippet; only "higher incentives slightly reduce no-shows" was. Rewrite superlatives down to what the snippet states.
- 2026-10-12 (usability-testing-cost-per-participant): A new silo had no sibling posts and no case study listing the hub in `services`, so the hub cap (2), TradeZella (the only case study whose body mentions testing), contact and one cross-hub link gave exactly 5 = BOFU minimum. Rule: when a dropped hub-fragment marker sits in an FAQ about user groups or participants, reuse that sentence for the single allowed cross-hub link (here ux-research-services, using the hub's own "discovery before testing" wording) rather than keeping a third hub fragment.
- 2026-10-12 (usability-testing-cost-per-participant, QA round 1): QA accepted 5 internal links on this new BOFU silo by the design-system-roi exception, but the round-1 report has to state the exception. Rule: when you finish at 5 on a BOFU post, write in `related` that the orchestrator exception applies (no siblings, no service case study, hub cap 2) and name what would make the 6th link (the first sibling post). Then QA and the orchestrator do not re-litigate it each round.
- 2026-10-13 (saas-onboarding): The brief marked 4 hub links "required" (root, #tours, #process, #cost), and the writer added a 5th in the Bottom line. None of them fell in the first 300 words: the earliest was in H2 5, about word 1,100. Rule: brief "required" labels do not override the 1–2 hub cap. Count words and put the root hub link on a sentence inside the first 300 words, even if no marker sits there (here, the end of H2 1's three-layers block). Keep a single #cost fragment as the late UP link, and leave the CTA sentence with /contact/ only.
- 2026-10-13 (saas-onboarding): The brief's Userpilot URL (`/saas-product-metrics/`) came back in a search, but its snippet never showed 37.5% / 62. The `/blog/user-activation-rate-benchmark-report-2024/` snippet did, and it says "62 B2B companies", not "SaaS". Later Userpilot posts cite 547 companies. Rule: for vendor benchmarks, cite the dated report page whose snippet shows the figure and its sample, and copy that sample's wording exactly.
- 2026-10-15 (product-discovery): The brief's design-sprint backlink suggested adding a `body` under a `format: "table"` hub section. SemanticSections.tsx renders that format as kind "compare", which shows `answer` (via Head/Html) but never `body`, so the link would have been invisible. Rule: before suggesting a hub backlink, check which fields the section's render kind outputs. For table/compare sections, append the link sentence to `answer` after `</strong>`. Pillar `faqs[].a` render HTML through Blocks.tsx `Faq`, but they also feed FAQPage schema.
- 2026-10-15 (product-discovery): The writer cited "a survey of 1,203 data management leaders" for Gartner's 60% AI-ready-data prediction. The release snippet showed two surveys (248 leaders for the 63% stat, 1,203 in July 2024), and no snippet tied either one to the 60%. Rule: when a press release cites several surveys, link only the prediction and cut the sample size from the sentence unless a snippet ties that sample to that exact figure. Flag the cut to the fact-checker.
- 2026-10-15 (product-discovery): This TOFU post had 11 unique source URLs and a new silo with no sibling posts and no industry pages (`content/industries/` holds only `_template.json`). Under the hub cap, that left 6 internal links. Rule: Glob `content/industries/*.json` before planning an industry link. When none exist, record the TOFU shortfall under the orchestrator exception in `related.internalCountException`. For externals, keep the central definition, the core framework and the number-carrying sources, and record the rest (verified) in `removedExternalLinks`.
- 2026-10-13 (saas-onboarding): The case-study link sentence said TradeZella "called for a phased" redesign, but tradezella.md never says phased. "Phased" comes from the service/pillar copy. Rule: check every adjective in a case-study sentence against the case-study file, and attach service-level qualifiers to the service link, not to the client.
- 2026-10-16 (user-onboarding-automation): The brief's W3C URL `focus-not-obscured.html` and its TechTarget URL `/searchcustomerexperience/definition/Product-Qualified-Lead-PQL` never came back in searches. The returned pages were `focus-not-obscured-minimum.html` and `/enterprise-software/definition/product-qualified-lead-PQL`. Rule: W3C Understanding slugs carry the SC's full short name ("-minimum", "-enhanced"), and TechTarget moves definitions between sections. Always cite the search-returned path, never the brief's path.
- 2026-10-16 (user-onboarding-automation): The writer's first hub marker sat at about word 395 (H2 1, after the scope list), not inside the first 300 words. Rule: count words to the first hub marker before replacing it. If it falls past 300, move the hub link into an intro sentence that already states the screen-first angle, and reword the old sentence without the hub head phrase so the phrase is never left as plain text.
- 2026-10-16 (user-onboarding-automation): The orchestrator asked for every named source marker to be linked (9 unique URLs), which conflicts with the 2–5 default. Rule: when the caller explicitly names the sources to link, follow the caller. Record `externalCountNote` in the brief's `related`, with the 5-link fallback set (regulation and number-carrying sources first), so QA or the orchestrator can trim in one step.
- 2026-10-15 (product-discovery, QA): after a hub link was dropped for the hub cap, its sentence kept the hub's head keyword as plain text ("Our AI product discovery workshop runs these checks"), against the brief note "only as link anchor text". Rule: when you de-link a sentence that names the hub, swap the head phrase for a variant ("our AI discovery workshop") in the same edit and log it in `droppedInternalMarkers`.
- 2026-10-16 (user-onboarding-automation): I kept 9 external links because the orchestrator asked for every source marker to be linked, and wrote the cap fallback only as a note. QA then had to apply the fallback (keep FTC, Gmail 81126, W3C 1.4.13, Braze, PAIR; de-link NN/g, W3C 2.4.11, Gmail 14229414, TechTarget as named citations such as "In its article on help and documentation, Nielsen Norman Group (NN/g)…" and "Google's sender guidelines FAQ defines a bulk sender as…"). Rule: when the count exceeds 5, apply the fallback in the post yourself, record the de-linked URLs in `related.removedExternalLinks`, and leave the over-cap version only as an option for the orchestrator.
